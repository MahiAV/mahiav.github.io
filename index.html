Preprint · Biological sequence distances

An Information Bottleneck in Pairwise Neural Distance Estimation: From Simulation Success to Biological Data

Mahtab Alizadeh Vandchali, Ziyun Guang, Nicolae Sapoval, Zhi Yan, Luay Nakhleh, Anastasios Kyrillidis

We studied why neural models can estimate evolutionary distances well in simulations yet struggle on biological data. We examined two possible causes: the information available in a pair of sequences and the mismatch between simulated and real data.

Across 418 mammalian and arthropod sub-alignments, the classical Jukes–Cantor method agrees more closely with IQ-TREE reference estimates than the tested neural methods. Those references are model-based estimates, not known true distances.

Preprint · 2026

A Local Theory of Gradient-Based Rank-One Recovery in Nonlinear Low-Rank Models

Mahtab Alizadeh Vandchali, Fangshuo Liao, Anastasios Kyrillidis

We studied how to choose the next low-rank update from a model’s gradient. The method selects one rank-one direction, fits the size of the update, and freezes it before moving to the next component.

For a single-layer Leaky-ReLU model with Gaussian inputs, we show when the gradient points toward what the model still needs to learn. The analysis tracks how nonlinear bias and sampling error affect recovery over successive updates.

TMLR 2026

One Rank at a Time: Cascading Error Dynamics in Sequential Learning

Mahtab Alizadeh Vandchali*, Fangshuo (Jasper) Liao*, Anastasios Kyrillidis

* Equal contribution

We developed a framework for tracking how errors carry from one rank-one update to the next. We tested its prediction that spending more compute on early updates can improve the final result under a fixed training budget.

Research directions

03

I’ve been a Graduate Research Assistant at Rice since 2024, working with Prof. Anastasios Kyrillidis.

i.

Sequential low-rank learning

We developed a sequential rank-one deflation framework to track how early approximation errors affect later updates. We tested the resulting compute-allocation guidance in synthetic experiments and LoRA adaptation studies.

ii.

Nonlinear rank-one recovery Preprint

We extended this line of work to nonlinear models, studying when the gradient reveals a useful low-rank direction and how much data is needed to estimate it reliably. Research summary ↑

iii.

Theory-guided fine-tuning Ongoing

I am using neural tangent kernel (NTK) theory to study low-rank adaptation (LoRA): how many components to learn and how much compute to spend on each. I’m currently developing the framework and experimental plan.
